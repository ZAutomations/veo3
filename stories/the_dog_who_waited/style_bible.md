# The Dog Who Waited - style bible

Preset: **Animal Kindness** (`animal-kindness`, niche)
Format: 16:9, 8s per clip, 10 clips, 80 seconds

## The look
Photorealistic 4K cinematic film, 35mm anamorphic lens quality, natural shallow depth of field, real-world lighting balance, physical micro-textures in fur, fabric and skin, restrained natural colour grading, premium streaming documentary quality

## Cast template
Photorealistic living creatures with true-to-life anatomy and proportions. ANIMALS: the exact breed or mix, natural build and size, real fur with individual strands visible, correct eye colour, and two unchanging physical markers. HUMANS: ordinary modern people with real skin texture, natural un-styled hair, plain worn clothing. NOT cartoon, NOT anime, NOT stylised, NOT anthropomorphic.

## Palette
Natural earth tones, warm amber and cool slate shadows, muted greens, overcast greys, soft golden-hour warmth

## Camera
Low intimate eye-level angles, slow push-ins, gentle handheld observation, steady tracking beside the animal, close-ups on eyes, ears and paws

## Direction
Ground every animal in real behaviour - head turns, ear tilts, weight shifts, shivering, a vigilant stance, cautious hesitation. An animal is never a passive prop and never behaves like a person. Humans are ordinary and tired, never villains and never saints. Put a hook - a visual anomaly or a striking emotional beat - inside the first two seconds. Deliver the lesson through action and through what it costs, never by saying it.

## Narrator
warm male voice, gentle and unhurried, quiet emotional weight, documentary warmth

## Never
no cartoon, anime or stylised anatomy, no anthropomorphic expressions, no fantasy or over-saturated look, no morphing or floating objects, no on-screen text, subtitles, captions, watermarks or UI, no dialogue, no graphic injury or on-screen death, no narration that states the moral

## Story shapes that suit this look
- a hook in the first two seconds, then one act of kindness, a separation, a long trial, an unexpected reversal, and a quiet payoff
- an animal and a person help each other once, then have to find each other again
- an animal makes a long journey home through weather and distance
- an ordinary day broken by one small kindness that costs the giver something
