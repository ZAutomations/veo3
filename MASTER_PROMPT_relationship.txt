 HOW TO USE THIS

   1. Copy everything below the line into ChatGPT (or Claude, or Gemini).
   2. Change only the TITLE and the ANGLE at the bottom of this header.
   3. Paste the JSON it returns into a new file:
          stories/<your_slug>/<your_slug>_story.json
   4. Run:   npm run agent:prompt -- stories/<your_slug>/<your_slug>_story.json

   The JSON it returns is already in the exact shape our pipeline reads, so
   step 4 works with no editing. If the model adds commentary outside the JSON,
   delete that - the file must be bare JSON and nothing else.

   This is the MANUAL path. `npm run story -- --preset relationship-dialogue`
   does the same thing automatically, and is the maintained one - it reads the
   look straight out of styles.json, so it can never fall behind it. Everything
   below is a hand-kept copy of that preset's look and rules: if you change the
   look THERE, change it here as well, or the two paths will quietly produce
   different films.

   This file is written for `relationship-dialogue`, the DRAWN preset - the
   2.5D semi-realistic medium. Its twin `relationship-dialogue-real` uses the
   same rules but a photorealistic look, which is owned by styles.json; that one
   has no hand-kept copy here, so use
   `npm run story -- --preset relationship-dialogue-real` for it. Do not simply
   swap "photorealistic" into the strings below - the preset changes more than
   the medium.

   YOU DESIGN BOTH PEOPLE. There is no fixed face: the two characters are yours
   for THIS story, drawn in the 2.5D semi-realistic medium below, and held
   consistent across the clips by their descriptions and their reference sheets.

   THE PLACE IS PER FILM, NOT HARDCODED. You choose ONE place to suit THIS story
   - a bed, a desk, a room, a bench - name it, describe it, and never leave it.
   There is no fixed tearoom any more.


 WHY THE RULES BELOW ARE STRICT

   Our code owns the video prompt. The AI only writes the CONTENT - what
   happens, who speaks, what they say. Every constraint here comes from a real
   failure: invented narration in a dialogue film, a clip too wordy to fit in 8
   seconds, a speaker who was never designed as a character and so had no face.

==========================================================
 THE FORMAT: A SPOKEN CONVERSATION, NOT A NARRATED FILM
==========================================================

This is the single most important rule, and everything else follows from it.

Two people sit together in one calm, beautiful room and talk to each other.
Everything the audience hears is said OUT LOUD by one of them to the other, in
their own voice, on screen.

    THERE IS NO NARRATOR. THERE IS NO VOICE-OVER. NOBODY DESCRIBES THE SCENE.
    If a human voice is not one of the two characters speaking to the other
    character, it does not exist in this video.

Do not write "we see", "the camera reveals", "she remembers" or any line that
only makes sense as narration. Every word must be something a person could say
to another person while sitting across a table from them.

==========================================================
 THE ONE PLACE (YOU CHOOSE IT ONCE - IT NEVER CHANGES)
==========================================================

This film happens in ONE place. Not one kind of place - one actual place, the
same one in every single clip, chosen to suit THIS story. A couple arguing about
money belongs at a desk; a couple reconnecting belongs on a bed; two people
holding their ground stand in a room. Pick the place the story needs, then never
leave it. The two of them are in it at the start and still in it at the end.

Choose the place yourself and write it THREE ways:

    "place_name"        - 1 or 2 words, what a person would call it: "Bedroom",
                          "Kitchen Table", "Park Bench". This becomes the image's
                          asset name - keep it short and plain.
    "place_description" - 45 to 70 words describing the fixed place ON ITS OWN,
                          with nobody in it. Say the furniture or landmarks, what
                          is on the walls or the ground, where the light comes
                          from, and the time of day. Write it so it reads
                          identically every time it is repeated.
    "place_prompt"      - a 40 to 60 word prompt for an image generator to make
                          that place as ONE image: the EMPTY place, no people and
                          no animals, a straight-on wide view, evenly lit, the
                          whole space readable. Same medium as the cast sheets.
                          Say that it is empty of people, and that there is no
                          text, no labels and no watermark.

Then copy "place_description" into EVERY clip EXACTLY, word for word, as the
first thing in "narrative_context". Everything you write after that sentence must
be new and different in each clip. That is where the film moves - not the place.

    DO NOT  describe the place again, or add furniture to it
    DO NOT  change the light, the time of day, or the weather
    DO NOT  move them to a cafe, a veranda, a garden, a memory or a flashback
    DO NOT  cut away to a street, a car, a phone screen, or another location

Move the CAMERA instead. Wide two-shot, over-the-shoulder single, medium
close-up, an insert shot on whatever is in their hands. A different angle on the
same place is what stops seven clips looking like one photograph. Changing the
place is what makes the finished video look broken.


==========================================================
 THE TWO CHARACTERS (YOU DESIGN BOTH)
==========================================================

This is a two-hander, so there are exactly two people in the film. There is no
fixed face: BOTH are yours to design for THIS story, and the story cannot be
told without them - a conversation has two sides, not a crowd. Nobody else
appears.

Design each of the two the same way: give them a name, and a 55-to-75-word
description that starts "Same <name> throughout - ". State age, build, hair,
face and skin tone, in one fixed multi-layered wardrobe that never changes, and
end with the NEVER guard restated as "NOT ..." so the model cannot drift -
exactly as the field spec below explains.

Both are drawn in the SAME medium - the 2.5D semi-realistic look in THE
AESTHETIC above: realistic adult proportions with gently idealised features,
large luminous eyes with long lashes, a small refined nose, soft blushing
cheeks, glossy semi-realistic skin, and hair rendered strand by strand. Indian
household styling - a saree or salwar-kameez whose colours, border and pallu are
stated once, henna mehndi on the hands and forearms, oxidised silver jhumka
earrings, glass bangles and a delicate necklace - fixed for that character and
never changed. NOT chibi, NOT super-deformed, NOT a flat cel-shaded cartoon,
NOT live-action, NOT a photograph, NOT 3D CGI.

Both hold their positions for the whole film and never leave the shot.

    The one laying down the rules holds the screen-LEFT position, angled facing
    right; the one hearing them holds screen-RIGHT, angled facing left. Whether
    the story has them seated or standing, they NEVER swap sides, NEVER leave
    the frame, and the camera NEVER crosses the axis between them. Neither of
    them changes clothes, hairstyle or jewellery between clips.


==========================================================
 THE SCRIPT STRUCTURE (30-60 SECONDS)
==========================================================

Build the exchange in this order:

   1. PROVOCATIVE HOOK
      An opening line that makes the viewer stop. A rule, a warning, a hard
      truth. Something they will want to argue with or send to someone.

   2. NUMBERED RULES / ADVICE
      Two or three pieces of real, specific advice. Name the behaviour. Avoid
      vague comfort like "communicate better".

   3. RELATABLE DOUBT / INTERRUPTION
      The one on the receiving end pushes back with the exact objection the
      audience is silently thinking. This is what stops the video feeling like a
      lecture. It must be a REAL objection, not a straw man.

   4. SHARP APHORISM
      One line worth repeating - poetic, short, surprising. This is the line
      people screenshot.

   5. WARM RESOLUTION
      Land it. Earned, not sentimental. The one hearing it does not have to
      agree; they have to UNDERSTAND.


==========================================================
 HARD CONSTRAINTS (THE VIDEO WILL NOT RENDER IF YOU BREAK THESE)
==========================================================

WORD BUDGET - the most common failure
    Each clip is 8 seconds. Aim for 18-24 spoken words per clip.
    NEVER exceed 30 words in a clip. Count them. Two short turns of 10-12 words
    each is ideal. A clip that overruns gets cut off mid-sentence.

SPEAKERS
    Only the two characters may speak. Nobody else. No off-screen voice. No "a
    passer-by says". Every speaker must be one of the two, and must be listed in
    that clip's "characters" array.

VISIBLE SPEECH
    The characters visibly speak - mouths move, they look at each other. Give
    the LISTENER something to do while the other talks: a teacup turned, a hand
    stilled, an eye-line that drops and comes back.

NO ON-SCREEN TEXT
    No subtitles, no captions, no titles, no watermarks. The images carry no
    text of any kind.
