PRESET ID: reference-wolf-dark-secrets-fable-dialogue

PRESET PURPOSE
This preset makes quiet conversations about relationships, betrayal, friendship, trust, rejection, feeling unappreciated or unaccepted, loyalty, boundaries, forgiveness, and self-worth. The topic is discussed in dialogue. It is not acted out as a plot or a physical event.

ORIGINALITY AND GENERATION SAFETY — REQUIRED
Wolf and Companion are independently designed fictional anthropomorphic animals for this channel. Use only the new character sheets created from the descriptions below. Give them clearly original facial markings, proportions, silhouettes, garments and color placement. The art direction is hand-painted 2D gouache and colored pencil on visible paper grain with irregular ink contours, a deep-teal, warm-ochre and cream palette, and soft lamplight. Do not use a named artist, studio or visual-property comparison. Keep video prompts focused on the current shot and their calm conversation; include no unrelated image ingredients. If a provider refuses a specific scene, inspect that scene's exact prompt and attached image references before making a substantive change. A refusal does not prove that the dialogue topic is the cause.

ROLES
Companion is the bear. He is the person who has experienced the hurt, is uncertain, or is asking for help. He opens with a sincere question or personal concern, such as: "I do so much for everyone. Why do they still act like I don't matter?"
Wolf is his wise, caring friend. Wolf listens attentively, acknowledges the feeling, asks a thoughtful follow-up when useful, and offers calm, practical perspective or a life lesson. Wolf does not lecture, blame, diagnose, or magically solve the problem. Companion remains an active part of the conversation and responds to Wolf's advice. Keep both characters kind and emotionally believable.

NO ACTION PLOT — REQUIRED
The entire story is a conversation between Wolf and Companion. Do not invent an event to demonstrate or prove the topic. Do not turn betrayal into a mystery or reveal, and do not show the betrayer. Do not use symbolic plot objects, secret letters, clues, evidence, ledgers, keys, gifts, contracts, property, chores, confrontations, rescues, journeys, departures, or other topic-related actions. Do not add villagers, partners, friends, witnesses, or other visible or speaking characters. No one rushes, grabs, builds, repairs, packs, confronts, or leaves.

They stay in ONE calm setting for the whole story, either sitting together or walking slowly side by side. Their actions are natural conversation behavior: listening, turning toward the speaker, a small paw gesture, a pause, a thoughtful expression, or a quiet nod. Show emotion through faces and attentive reactions, never through a staged plot. The hook is Companion's spoken question or confession, never a visual stunt. Keep setting, character designs and wardrobe consistent. Clips 1–6 are a conversation between Wolf and Companion. In clips 7 and 8, Wolf turns toward the camera and speaks directly to the audience; this is the only direct-to-camera section. Companion may sit quietly beside him or be out of frame. No extra characters appear.

FIXED STORY ARC — STANDARD 8 CLIPS
Clips 1–4 — Companion explains the hurt, betrayal, friendship problem, or feeling unappreciated in a natural conversation. Wolf listens, empathizes, asks a useful question, and briefly reflects what he understands. Companion is the person facing the problem.
Clips 5–6 — Wolf gives Companion focused, caring, practical counsel. Explain the life lesson through useful steps, boundaries or ways to think; do not lecture harshly or repeat generic morals. Companion may respond briefly.
Clips 7–8 — Wolf addresses the audience directly. Explain what viewers can do to avoid falling into this situation and what practical steps to take if they are already experiencing it. Keep this warm, useful and concise. Do not make these final clips a conversation with Companion. For durations other than eight clips, retain the same three phases and reserve the final two clips for Wolf's audience advice; use early clips for the issue conversation and middle clips for counsel to Companion.
